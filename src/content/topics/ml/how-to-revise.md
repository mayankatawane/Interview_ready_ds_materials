## 1. How every topic page is laid out

1. **What it is**, in plain words, with an everyday analogy (purple boxes).
2. **Why it exists**: the problem it solves.
3. **How it trains**, step by step, usually with small numbers you can check by hand.
4. **How it predicts** on a new row, kept separate from training.
5. **The maths**, and right after every formula a blue box explaining **each symbol**.
6. **Hyperparameters**, one by one: what each controls and what happens when you raise or lower it.
7. **Strengths, weaknesses and when to use it**, with real use cases.
8. **Interview traps** (amber boxes): the tricky follow-ups.
9. **Code**: simple, no pipelines.
10. **Say it** (green box): a 30-second spoken answer.
11. **Most asked interview questions** at the bottom, with answers.
12. **My notes**: your own notes for the topic, saved on your phone.

Interactive visuals sit inside the pages. Drag the sliders; they're there to build intuition you can describe in words.

## 2. Revision plan for the day before an interview

| When | What | How |
|---|---|---|
| Pass 1 (60–75 min) | Read and play | Read the "What it is" and "How it trains" sections and the purple and amber boxes. Play with each visual for a minute. |
| Pass 2 (45 min) | Questions | At the bottom of each topic, say your answer **out loud** before opening it. Mark **Knew it** or **Review again**. |
| Pass 3 (20 min) | Flashcards and quiz | Questions tab → Flashcards, then the Quiz. |
| Last 15 min | Review pile | Questions tab → Review pile only. Then rest. |

Turn on **Recall mode** (the eye icon at the top) for pass 2. It blurs the green "Say it" answers until you tap them, so you have to answer first.

## 3. The answer structure that scores well

:::remember
1. Answer the exact question in **one sentence**.
2. Explain **how it works**: intuition first, then the formula if useful.
3. Give **one example from your own work**, with a number.
4. Say the **trade-off** or when you would *not* use it.

If you don't know something, say what you would check and how. Never skip a question.
:::

## 4. Ways candidates lose marks

- Naming a tool without saying **why** you chose it.
- Answering a nearby question instead of the one asked (e.g. drifting to "balance the data" when asked how multiclass logistic regression works).
- Going silent while coding: think aloud, even if the code is incomplete.
- Saying "accuracy" for an imbalanced problem.
- Not knowing the numbers from your own projects.
